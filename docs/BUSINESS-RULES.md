# Goal Cascade — Business Rules

The rules of the final design, by entity. This is the contract the UI implements; the API enforces the same.

## Goal

- A goal is a tree node: `title` (required), optional one-line `why`, `horizon`, `pulse`, `periodKey` (the period it sits in), optional `parentId`, any number of children.
- Horizons: **Life → Yearly → Quarterly → Monthly → Weekly** (rank 0–4).
  - Life goals have no parent and no period.
  - A child's horizon must be **strictly shorter** than its parent's; nesting can never exceed Life › Yearly › Quarterly › Monthly › Weekly. Weekly goals cannot have sub-goals.
  - Levels may be skipped. A weekly goal usually hangs off a monthly one, but it may hang off any longer horizon — including a life goal directly.
- **Period**: every non-life goal sits in exactly one period of its own horizon — a year, a quarter, a month, or a week (its Monday). The period is what each lens filters on, so it is a canonical key, not free text; the label you see (`2026`, `Q3 2026`, `Sep 2026`, `Week of 7 Sep`) is rendered from it. A goal's period is never checked against its parent's: a week that straddles a month boundary is ordinary, not an error.
- **Only monthly and weekly goals hold tasks.** Not quarterly, not yearly, not life — and not a quarterly goal that happens to have no monthly children yet. The rule is the horizon, nothing else. A month is the longest deadline you can put on a piece of work here; past that it is a goal, or it is in the backlog.
- **Create**: title required. There are two ways in, and both already know everything except the title.
  - **From a lens**: the heading names the horizon, so there is no horizon picker; the period is a read-only chip taken from the lens you are standing in, so creating a goal into a period you are not looking at is impossible. The only choice is the parent, and the picker offers legal parents only — goals with a longer horizon, in the enclosing period. It starts on the **nearest** one: the deepest goal whose period contains the new goal's, so a monthly goal opens on that quarter's goal rather than on a life goal. Inside the form it is a single row naming that choice; opening it gives you the goals one horizon at a time, starting at the nearest horizon that has any, and typing searches across all of them at once.
  - **From a goal's own page**: every goal that can hold children — that is, everything but a weekly one — lists its sub-goals whether it has any or not, with a "+ Sub-goal" line under them. The parent is the goal you are standing on. The horizon is whatever is legally shorter, and where only one is (a monthly goal, which can only hold weeks) you are not asked; where several are, it starts at the next one down. The period follows the horizon — the current one, or the parent's first if the parent starts later. Type a title and press Enter. "More…" opens the full form with all of it carried across, including what you have typed so far.
- **Nothing is created into a past period, and nothing is moved into one.** Past periods stay readable exactly as they were: planning does not rewrite history. There is no limit in the other direction — you can set goals as far ahead as you like.
  - A past period is closed to new plan and to nothing else. You can still correct a title, complete a task that was live that week, or uncheck one.
- **Move (re-parent)**: valid targets have a longer horizon (lower rank) and are not the goal itself or one of its descendants — invalid targets are shown disabled with the reason ("horizon conflict" / "its own descendant"). Children move with the goal. A preview of the new path is shown before confirming.
- **Re-plan**: changes the target period to a contextual next period (Monthly → next months, Quarterly → next quarters, Yearly → next year) with an **optional** one-line reason. No mandatory fields. A **weekly goal is not re-plannable**: it *is* a week, and moving it would restate what a past week held. An intention that did not happen carries forward through its open tasks, or is written again for the new week.
- **Pulse**: one of On track / At risk / Rethink, per goal.
- A goal is never "done". There is no completion state, no progress bar and no `N of M` at any horizon. Whether the week went well is answered by looking at that week; `pulse` is the one self-reported signal.
- Quiet signal only: a life-goal card shows "N tasks carrying · oldest W weeks" when open carried tasks exist under it. There are no audit pages, review wizards, or reports.

## Lenses

- The tree is not how you navigate. There are **five lenses, one per horizon**, and each is a flat list of every goal at that horizon across the whole account — not a branch you walk into. The hierarchy still exists; it shows up as context on an item, not as a path.
- **The lens is a row of tabs** — `Life  Yearly  Quarterly  Monthly  Weekly`, left to right, one tap to change. The strip scrolls sideways if a narrow screen cannot fit all five, and no label is ever shortened: the words stay whole and the window moves over them. It sits directly under the top-right cluster, with the period control on the line below it, and both stay pinned to the top while the list scrolls.
- Every lens except Life is scoped to **one period**, with a chevron each way: Yearly by year, Quarterly by quarter, Monthly by month, Weekly by week. Life is simply all of them. The two chevrons are the whole period control — there is no picker, and the label between them is text rather than a control. There is no cap in either direction. When a later period holds work, a dot sits on the forward chevron, so a goal set three months out is not invisible until you get there.
- **A flat list, with no grouping and no filtering.** Every lens is one list of cards, at every horizon. There are no filter pills and no life-goal group headers. Each card carries the orientation instead, as one muted line: `under <life goal>` — the life goal its chain reaches, at every horizon, always. An item whose chain reaches no life goal says `Not under a Life goal yet` and offers to put it under one; those sort last.
- **The order is the reading order of the account**: the items of one life line together, the lines in the order those life goals were written, and the items inside a line in the order they were written. Nothing reorders itself when you change a pulse or tick a checkbox.
- The **open-task count** — what is visible in the selected week in the Weekly lens, and in the current week everywhere else — sits on that life goal's own card in the **Life lens**, and nowhere else: it is a fact about a line, and on a flat list it would otherwise be printed once per goal in that line. The Life lens is the roster; the other four are working lists. The only other numbers on a lens are the ones you wrote down yourself, on a task that carries a measure.
- **The `why` line shows on the Life lens only**, clamped to one line. On a working list it is written once, never changes, repeats down every card from the same line, and is one tap away on the goal's own page — so the card spends that line on `under <life goal>` instead and comes out shorter than before.
- The Monthly lens shows a monthly goal's own tasks, the way the Weekly lens shows a weekly goal's. Those are the two lenses with work in them.
- **A period is the whole weeks it holds, and the lens says so.** A week belongs to the month, quarter and year its **Monday** falls in, so `Sep 2026` is the four weeks beginning 7, 14, 21 and 28 September — not the 1st to the 30th. The title carries the span underneath it: `Sep 2026` over `Mon 7 Sep – Sun 4 Oct`, with the years spelled out when the two ends fall in different ones. A week's own label already names its Monday and needs nothing added.
- **When this week is somewhere else, the lens says that too.** For the first few days of a month, a quarter or a year, the period you open on does not hold the week you are living in — on Tue 1 Sep the Monthly lens is on `Sep 2026` and this week began Mon 31 Aug, in August. That reads as a bug and is not one, so a quiet line says `This week is in Aug 2026` with one tap to go there. It replaces the past/future badge in the same row rather than adding one, and it never shows when the two agree.
- **One create action.** A lens has a single `+ Goal` in the top-right cluster and nothing inside the list. Its sheet asks for a title, an optional why, the **horizon** — five chips, defaulting to the lens you are on — and the goal it hangs under. The period follows the horizon and is never typed: changing the horizon moves it to the period holding where you already were, re-scopes the list of legal parents, and clears a parent that no longer fits, saying so rather than changing it quietly. A life goal has neither. If you create at a horizon other than the one on screen, the app takes you there, so nothing is created and then invisible.
- **Zooming** between lenses keeps where you are. Zoom out and you get the period containing the one you were in. Zoom in and you get the period containing **today** when the one you were in contains today — from Q3 in August you land in August, not July — and otherwise the first sub-period.
- A future period is badged with its own horizon and nothing more — `Future month — planning ahead`, `Future week — planning ahead`. A past one reads `Past week — still editable`. The current period is unbadged. Work that has not come due is never styled as late.
- Bottom tabs: **Goals · + · Learnings**. The lens strip is not one of these — it lives inside the Goals screen, and the bottom bar stays a top-level destination switcher. The **+** opens the Add-to-Backlog drawer ("View Backlog →" inside reaches the full page).

## The weekly lens

- A weekly goal is an ordinary goal with `horizon = 'Weekly'`, written for one week. Several can sit under the same monthly goal in the same week — that is how a week holds more than one intention.
- A weekly goal shows up in a week's lens if it **belongs to that week**, or if it **still has open tasks carrying into it**. The second kind is shown separately, below the week's own goals, labelled with the week it was written for and oldest first, so it never reads as this week's plan.
- Nothing ages out of that carried list. A goal with work open for ten weeks appears for ten weeks, and the growing red chip on the task is the only thing that escalates. Hiding it would lose work quietly, which is the one thing carrying exists to prevent.
- Below both of those sits **this month**: the month tasks of whichever month this week belongs to, grouped by the monthly goal they hang on. A week belongs to its Monday's month, so on Wednesday 2 September the week beginning Mon 31 August shows **August's** month tasks. It sits last because the week's own plan is what the week is for.
- **Nothing in that month band wears a late label** — no red chip, no grey "since", no badge. A month task you have not got to in week two is not late; its deadline is the end of the month. The week has no standing to say otherwise.
- Everything that used to be on the Tasks page happens here: the week switcher, completing, unchecking, the three exits, carry labels, backlog pulls.
- A weekly goal written well in advance shows a muted "planned N weeks ago" once its week arrives, from two weeks out. Nothing is asked of you when a planned week arrives — the plan is just the plan.
- **Repeat last week** sits once at the foot of the week's list and copies the previous week's weekly goals — every line's — into the selected week as ordinary new goals: same titles, no tasks, nothing linking them. There is no recurring-goal machinery behind it, and a repeat that would overflow the week's cap is refused whole rather than applied in part.

## Task

- Lives under a **monthly or a weekly goal**. Created from: `+ Task` on either, a Backlog pull, or the + drawer with "Add to this week instead". On a weekly goal that does not exist yet, the same sheet takes a one-line weekly-goal title and creates both at once — one step, not two. On a monthly goal there is nothing to create first: the task goes on the goal you tapped, in the month you are looking at, and the screen does not move — or you name one of that month’s weeks instead, and it becomes a week task there.
- Fields: `title` (required), `cond` done-condition (**optional**), optional `desc` description and external `links`, and an optional `measure` — the number this task carries.
- **Period model**: a task belongs to one period, at one scope. A task on a weekly goal has a **week**; a task on a monthly goal has a **month**. `origin` is the period it was created into, taken from its goal and then fixed; `done` is the period it was completed in, or null. Weeks start **Monday**, and a month is the whole weeks whose Monday falls in it. The goal says what the work is *for*; the task's own period says *when it was live*, and only the task's period decides where it shows up.
  - An **open** task is visible in every period ≥ its origin, at its own scope — it auto-carries with no prompt, no wizard and no move operation. A week task carries into next week; a month task carries into next month. Its goal comes with it.
  - A **done** task is visible only in the period it was completed in, with a muted "Done Fri 28 Aug" date.
  - You can create work in a period that has not started yet; it is invisible until that period arrives and never carries a late label before then. A task can only be **completed** in a period that has already begun, and never in a past one it was created into.
- **Park it in a week.** A month task can be moved onto a weekly goal for a specific week — "this is the week I'll do it" — and moved back to its month again. It keeps its title, its condition, its description, its links, its history and its whole timeline; only the goal and the period change. It is a write, it is logged, and it is not a fourth exit: the task is still open and still yours to finish. The weeks you can pick are the weeks of the month the task is **in now**, which for a task that has carried is not the month it started in. Moving back goes to the month of the monthly goal above it and nowhere else, so once that month has ended there is no legal month to move back to and the offer is simply not there.
- **Carry labels** (in the viewed period, age counted against today, so a plan never ages, and always in the task's own unit):
  - age 1 → gray "since Mon 24 Aug" for a week, "since Aug" for a month
  - age ≥ 2 → red chip "N weeks · since 10 Aug" / "N months · since Aug" (the only escalation; no popups).
  - age 0 or below → nothing. Work planned for a future period has a negative age; it is early, not late.
  - A month task shows **no label at all** inside a week (see the weekly lens). The chip fires between months, in the Monthly lens, where the unit means something.
- **Exits** (exactly three):
  1. **Complete** — checkbox, any period that has begun (past periods stay fully interactive).
  2. **Move to Backlog** — the item goes to the backlog of the nearest goal that can hold one: for a week task the monthly parent, for a month task the goal it is already on. It keeps its description and links, with a "from week of …" or "from Sep 2026" note. It has to leave the period; that is the point of the exit.
  3. **Cancel** — task dropped.
  - Move and Cancel use a lightweight confirm with an **optional** reason. Fast and guilt-free; nothing mandatory.
- **Unchecking** a completed task (any period) logs "Unchecked" and offers a skippable inline "Update the done-condition?" edit; the task then carries into the current period with its original origin, and its goal reappears alongside it.
- Tapping a task opens its **own page**, not a drawer — it is a linkable thing.
- **Activity**: a read-only, auto-logged timeline per task, newest first — Created (with source: added to a goal / Backlog pull / + drawer), Carried to …, Parked in the week of … / Moved to Sep 2026, Renamed (old → new, truncated), Done-condition edited (old → new, truncated), Description updated, Link added, Measure added / edited / removed, Completed, Unchecked, Moved to Backlog / Canceled (with reason if given). Never requires user input. **A recorded value is not a timeline entry** — the readings are their own list, on the same page, and copying them in would drown everything else.

## Measure

- A task can carry a number. Most don't, and a task without one is an ordinary checkbox, unchanged in every way.
- Two shapes: a **counter** you add to ("+3"), and a **gauge** you set ("= 78.5"). A checkbox is neither; it is the absence of a measure, not a counter that stops at one.
- One triple — **start / current / target** — plus an optional unit you type yourself: `leads`, `kg`, `reps`. The unit is a word, never parsed and never converted.
- **Direction is implied.** Target above start counts up; target below start counts down. There is no direction to set, because the two numbers already say it. Progress is `(current − start) / (target − start)` either way. A target equal to the start is refused: it names no movement.
- **The target is optional**, and a measure without one is a real thing, not a broken one — an AMRAP set whose reps go up and down is tracked, has a history, and has no percentage and no finish line. That is correct.
- **History is a list of readings** — a value and a time — added to, never edited, and **each one deletable**, because a mistyped 240 for 24 has to be fixable. Deleting one takes the current value back to the one before it. The task page shows a sparkline and the recent values.
- **Readings follow the task, not the week.** A task carries across weeks and months, and can be parked and unparked; its numbers come with it every time. A history that reset each Monday would be worth nothing.
- **Reaching the target does not complete the task, and completing the task does not record a value.** You decide when it is done, and you decide what the number was. A task can be completed at 12 of 15, and that is the truth of it.
- **The app shows you what you recorded and never computes a verdict.** No pace, no projection, no trend line, no "at this rate", no on-track colour, no streak, no completion rate, no rolling a month's target up out of its weeks. Your own readings played back are your data; a judgement about whether you are behind is the app's opinion of you, and this product does not have those.
- Two things deliberately left out: **ratios** — a numerator over a denominator is the app deriving a number, which is the line above; and **checklists** — "visit these 5 clients" is five named things, so it is five tasks or five lines of a description, not a count of five.
- And no recurrence. A gauge is overwritten whenever you measure it, so "any day" needs no day. "15 leads daily" is a counter you bump, and "daily" belongs in the title.

## Backlog item

- Deferred future work attached to any **yearly/quarterly/monthly** goal — never a life goal, and never a weekly goal, because a backlog item has no week and a weekly goal would give it one.
- Fields: `title` + captured date; optional description and links. **No** checkbox, done-condition, due date, or status. Rendered clearly differently from tasks.
- **A backlog item and a month task both sit on a monthly goal, and they are not the same thing.** Backlog is "maybe, someday"; a month task is "yes, this month". What makes that real rather than a feeling is that **a backlog item has no period at all** — which is why it never appears in a week or a month, never ages, and never has anything to be late for. A month task has a month, so it appears everywhere that month does. Moving between them is one tap in either direction, so neither has to do the other's job.
- Created from: the global **+** drawer (goal defaults to last used), a goal detail "+ Add", or a task moved out of a period.
- **The only way backlog becomes work**: "Add to this month" or "Add to this week" → opens the task-create sheet pre-filled; on save the item is **converted** (removed), never duplicated. It is the same sheet as any other new task, so anything you set in it — a number included — is part of the same save. "This month" lands it on the monthly goal it is already attached to, with nothing to resolve. "This week" lands it on a weekly goal for the target week: if two qualify you choose, and if none exists the sheet takes a weekly-goal title and makes one.
- A weekly goal's page lists the backlog items sitting on the goals above it, ready to pull in.
- Other actions: Move to another goal (any yearly/quarterly/monthly goal), Delete.
- Grouped by life goal › sub-goal (branch path). Within a goal the order is yours: newest first until you rearrange it, then whatever order you put it in. Reordering works by dragging and by keyboard alike. A life goal's detail screen shows a **read-only aggregate** of its descendants' items, each labeled with its goal.

## Learning

- A short insight that might change the plan; optional life-goal tag; not a journal, not a task.
- Tap actions: **Attach to a goal** (re-tag), **Discard**.
- A learning that changed the plan carries a "changed the plan" badge.

## Navigation & system

- Bottom tabs: Goals · **+** (opens the Add-to-Backlog drawer) · Learnings. The lens tabs and the period control live in the Goals header, pinned together above the list.
- Lenses, goal pages, task pages, the Backlog page and Learnings are all real addresses — back, forward and a pasted link all work. Capture drawers and confirm sheets are not; nobody wants a URL for those.
- Every page: consistent top-right cluster — theme toggle (light/dark) + one primary action.
- Removed entirely: the goal tree as a navigation surface, the Tasks page, the weekly-planning page, the Ideas parking lot, goal-filter pills, weekly review wizards, push flows with mandatory reasons, audit-trail views, week reports, carry-count flags, and any completion rate, streak, pace, projection, trend line, on-track state or period summary. The only numbers in the product are the open-task count on a life goal's card in the Life lens, the carrying line on a life goal, the backlog count on a goal, the carry chip on a task, and the values you recorded yourself on a measure. Every one of those is something that exists or something you wrote down. Nothing in here is a score.
- Goals carry no targets. The number in "Sign at least 2 clients" lives in the title today; the place for it now is a measurable task on that quarter's monthly goal, where it can actually accumulate. A target on a goal would want a progress bar on a goal, and then a rate, and then a report.
